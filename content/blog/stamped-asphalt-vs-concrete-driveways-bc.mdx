If you've been pricing a driveway refresh, you've run into the same comparison most BC homeowners eventually do: stamped asphalt or stamped concrete. Both produce a finished surface with pattern, colour, and curb appeal. Both carry a premium over plain black asphalt. Past that, the two are different products with different performance profiles, and in BC, the climate makes one of them a noticeably better bet.

We install stamped asphalt exclusively. We're biased, and I'll be upfront about why. But the technical case is also clear.

## The short version

Stamped concrete looks great on install day and for about five years afterward. In BC's freeze-thaw climate, the joints between the concrete slabs inevitably crack. Once water gets into a crack, every winter expands it. By year ten, most stamped concrete driveways in the Lower Mainland show pattern-level fractures that can't be patched invisibly.

Stamped asphalt (we install StreetPrint) behaves differently. Asphalt is structurally flexible. It moves with the freeze-thaw cycle instead of fighting it. The manufacturer publishes a 10–20 year service life for StreetPrint, and its StreetBond colour coat is refreshed rather than replaced when it dulls.

## What concrete does well

There's no question concrete feels premium under foot. It's rigid, the pattern is sharp, and in a dry climate it can look showroom-new for a decade.

A dry climate is the load-bearing part of that sentence.

## What happens in BC specifically

Concrete in BC deals with:

- Repeated freeze-thaw cycles through a Fraser Valley winter
- Saturated ground conditions for most of the rainy season
- Hairline cracking from thermal expansion that widens every year
- Surface scaling when de-icer chemicals react with the cement matrix

Asphalt in BC deals with the same conditions, but the material itself flexes. A stamped asphalt surface moves micro-millimetres up and down with temperature changes and settles back. Concrete can't do that: it's a rigid slab. When it wants to move and can't, it cracks.

## Install cost

Price depends on the site, the base and the pattern, so the useful comparison is two written quotes for the same driveway. The install labour is different (concrete is pour-and-stamp, asphalt is pave-and-texture).

Where the two part ways is in the years after.

Concrete typically needs a partial repair at some point. A stamped concrete driveway that develops a run of cracks is difficult to fix invisibly: matching the stamp pattern, colour, and aggregate exposure on a patch is nearly impossible. The honest options are live with visible repairs or redo the full driveway.

Stamped asphalt needs its colour coat refreshed to restore the saturation (the manufacturer publishes an 8+ year life cycle for the StreetBond coat). That's maintenance, not repair. The pattern stays intact.

## What we install

StreetPrint is the system we use for stamped asphalt driveways. Its manufacturer publishes how it performs under traffic, snowplows and de-icing salt, which is the performance that matters here. The patterns we install most often on BC properties are ashlar slate, herringbone brick, and cobblestone. They read as premium hardscape from the street and they hold up under real vehicle weight.

## Both products need base work

The biggest predictor of whether any decorative driveway holds up isn't the product on top. It's the condition of what's underneath.

We don't quote a stamped surface until we've walked the existing driveway, checked drainage, probed the asphalt base condition, and flagged any settlement issues. If the base is compromised, we say so. Installing a premium surface over a failing base just means the failure shows up sooner.

## If you're deciding

For a BC property in the Lower Mainland or on Vancouver Island, stamped asphalt is the more climate-appropriate choice. Concrete isn't wrong: it just demands a climate concrete was designed for, and we don't have that climate.
