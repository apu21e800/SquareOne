The water play feature at Confederation Park in Burnaby was revitalized to a design by Space2place landscape architecture — an installation using StreetBond® 150 that presented an exciting design opportunity for the project team.

Confederation Park is a significant Burnaby park with heavy summer use. The water play feature draws families from across the area during the peak season, with children running barefoot across the surface for hours at a time in conditions of full summer UV and constant water saturation.

## StreetBond 150 for water play surfaces

StreetBond 150 is our preferred specification for spray pad and water play applications. The product is formulated for environments where the surface will be perpetually wet, subject to intense UV exposure during operating hours, and trafficked by barefoot users — a combination that eliminates many coating systems from consideration.

The key performance requirements for a water play surface:

**Slip resistance** — children running on a wet surface need consistent traction. StreetBond 150 incorporates a fine aggregate finish that maintains grip even on saturated surfaces.

**Colour stability** — the visual design of a water play area is part of the experience. Colours that fade to grey within a season undermine the investment. StreetBond 150's UV-stable formulation holds saturation through full BC summer conditions.

**Comfort** — the finish has to grip without being rough on bare feet, which is a matter of the aggregate and how it is embedded, and something we set for spray pad work specifically.

## For a parks department renewing a water feature

- **Treat the recoat as a redesign.** A renewal is the moment to change the layout, as Space2place did here, rather than to repeat the old one in fresh colour.
- **Match the aggregate to bare feet.** Ask for the spray pad finish specifically; a court finish grips harder than a pad needs.
- **Plan the closure before the season.** A pad coated in the shoulder months cures through the closure and opens on schedule; one coated in July loses the summer.
