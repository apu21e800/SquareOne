The David Foster Walkway runs along Victoria's Inner Harbour — a route that connects residents and visitors to key destinations along one of BC's most-photographed waterfronts. We were brought in to renovate the pathway surface using StreetBond® 150 coatings in a High Visibility Safety Blue specification.

The project replaced a worn asphalt surface with a bright, durable coating system that reads clearly in Victoria's grey winter light and holds its colour through the salt-air and UV exposure characteristic of a foreshore location.

## What the photograph shows

The lede photograph is the walkway in use on a sunny day. The path runs along the harbour edge in a saturated blue, with a scalloped wave pattern in a darker blue repeating down its length; a chain-and-bollard rail separates it from the water on one side, and the ordinary grey asphalt of the causeway lies on the other. People are walking it, a seaplane operator's building stands alongside, and the dome of the Legislature stands at the far end of the frame. The blue reads from the length of the harbour, which is what "high visibility" means on a shared path.

## High Visibility Blue on an asphalt path

The Safety Blue colourway was selected for maximum visibility — both for cyclists and pedestrians navigating the shared path, and for the visual contribution the surface makes to the harbour experience.

StreetBond coatings are applied directly to the existing asphalt substrate. No demolition, no resurfacing — the existing pavement becomes the base for a new colour and texture layer. The finish incorporates a fine aggregate for slip resistance, which is non-negotiable on a waterfront path that will be wet for months at a time.

The manufacturer of StreetBond publishes an 8+ year life cycle for the coating and describes the end of it as a refresh rather than a replacement: a worn surface is recoated, not rebuilt.

## The system

StreetBond is installed by Square One under our [decorative coatings](/services/decorative-coatings) service. The manufacturer's published facts: a water-based acrylic pavement coating that bonds to asphalt and to concrete; more than 50 standard colours plus custom mixing; an anti-skid aggregate for wet surfaces; a UV-stable finish; and retroreflective options for owners who need the path to read at night. The [StreetBond product page](/products/streetbond) has the specification, and the [parks and paths gallery](/applications/parks-paths) shows the coating on greenways and walkways across the Island and the Lower Mainland.

## The walkway network

The David Foster Walkway is part of a broader harbour pathway network that Victoria has been building out over the past decade. Projects like this one demonstrate what's possible when pathway surfaces are treated as part of the urban design — not just functional asphalt, but finished civic infrastructure that signals care for public space.

Victoria is where a good deal of our Island work lives: the record holds StreetBond walkways at Ogden Point and Shipspoint, StreetPrint® at Beacon Hill Park and Hillside Mall, and the [Victoria High School whorl and canoes](/projects/victoria-high-school-whorl-canoes) in DecoMark and StreetBond.

## For a municipality or harbour authority

- **Pick one colour and commit to it.** A single saturated field with a simple repeat, as here, reads as wayfinding along the whole route; several colours read as decoration.
- **Coat, don't rebuild.** Where the asphalt is sound but tired, StreetBond over the existing surface avoids demolition and keeps the path open longer; the site visit confirms the substrate.
- **Schedule for the dry.** A waterfront path is wet for much of the year, and the coating needs a dry window to cure.
