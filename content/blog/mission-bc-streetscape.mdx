Mission, BC — a district working through a multi-year downtown renewal program — specified StreetPrint stamped asphalt and StreetBond 150 in Pewter for a streetscape install completed in April 2025. We installed the combined scope across the pedestrian crossings and decorative surfaces of the downtown core.

This is a case study on how a smaller BC municipality uses decorative pavement to signal that its downtown is worth visiting.

## The Downtown Renewal Context

Mission's downtown has been through the cycle that many BC resource-town downtowns have been through — mid-century prosperity, late-century decline as retail moved to suburban plazas, and a slow early-twenty-first-century effort to reinvest in the walkable core. The District's renewal program committed capital to streetscape, facade improvement, and public-realm projects intended to make the downtown a place people choose to walk through rather than a place they drive past.

Pavement is one of the most visible levers in that kind of renewal. A downtown that has worn asphalt and painted parking stripes reads as neglected even if the storefronts are active. A downtown with stamped-asphalt crossings and decorative coating fields reads as a place that the municipality has chosen to invest in. The storefronts haven't changed. The pavement has — and the read of the whole corridor shifts.

## Why StreetPrint and StreetBond Together

The spec combined two products for complementary reasons.

**StreetPrint stamped asphalt** — used for the pedestrian crossings and the textured field treatments. StreetPrint imprints a relief pattern into freshly laid or heated asphalt, giving the pavement the appearance of cobblestone, brick, or flagstone without the maintenance and disruption of actual unit pavers. It is the standard approach when the design intent is "heritage streetscape" on a budget that excludes full unit-paver installation.

**StreetBond 150 in Pewter** — used for colour wash across the stamped surfaces and for decorative field treatments adjacent to the crossings. Pewter is a neutral grey with warm undertones that reads as slate or aged stone under BC overcast light. It is a palette choice that reinforces the heritage-streetscape intent without committing to a saturated colour that would date more quickly.

The combined install gives Mission a downtown pavement treatment that reads as intentional and considered, with durability profiles that match the municipality's long-cycle capital-planning horizon.

## The Install Scope

Streetscape work happens inside an active downtown. The corridor has to stay open to retail, transit, and pedestrian traffic through most of the install window. We phased the work across multiple nights and staged weekend blocks, coordinating with the District's public-works team and the downtown BIA on messaging to retailers.

StreetPrint installation requires the asphalt to be at working temperature. In April on the BC mainland, ambient temperatures support fresh-asphalt stamping and heated-asphalt stamping on separate schedules, and we ran the install across a window that let both approaches happen without compromising the stamp quality.

StreetBond 150 went down after the StreetPrint cured. The Pewter colour wash was applied across the stamped surfaces using rollers and squeegees, with edge cutting on the transitions to adjacent unstamped pavement. Cure windows were tracked against the scheduled reopening of each corridor section.

## What Pewter Does in the BC Light

Colour specification on streetscape work is a technical conversation even when the outcome looks like an aesthetic choice. Pewter reads differently under the four types of light a Mission streetscape will encounter:

- **Summer midday**: warm grey, reads almost tan against the stamped texture
- **Overcast winter**: cool grey, reads as slate
- **Wet surface**: darkens significantly, reads as a near-charcoal
- **Evening LED streetlight**: neutral grey, holds its value across the full block

A saturated colour — red, green, yellow — would have read differently in each of those conditions in ways that would date the install. Pewter holds across all four, which is why it is the palette choice we recommend for BC streetscape work where the design intent is long-cycle neutrality.

## What the Corridor Reads Like Now

Downtown Mission now has a pavement treatment that matches the scale and ambition of its heritage architecture. A visitor walking the corridor registers the stamped texture underfoot, the colour field across the surface, and the crosswalk treatments at the intersections — and the read is of a downtown that has been curated rather than a downtown that has been left to wear out.

From the District's perspective, the install is one piece of a multi-year commitment. Facade grants, streetfurniture replacement, and public-realm programming sit alongside the pavement work. The combined effect is what restores a downtown's trajectory, and the pavement carries its portion of that work for the fifteen-plus-year service horizon of the StreetBond coating.
