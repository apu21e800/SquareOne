Mission, BC — a district working through a multi-year downtown renewal program — specified StreetPrint stamped asphalt and StreetBond 150 in Pewter for a streetscape install completed in April 2025. We installed the combined scope across the pedestrian crossings and decorative surfaces of the downtown core.

This is a case study on how a smaller BC municipality uses decorative pavement to signal that its downtown is worth visiting.

## Why StreetPrint and StreetBond Together

The spec combined two products for complementary reasons.

**StreetPrint stamped asphalt** — used for the pedestrian crossings and the textured field treatments. StreetPrint imprints a relief pattern into freshly laid or heated asphalt, giving the pavement the appearance of cobblestone, brick, or flagstone without the maintenance and disruption of actual unit pavers. It is the standard approach when the design intent is "heritage streetscape" on a budget that excludes full unit-paver installation.

**StreetBond 150 in Pewter** — used for colour wash across the stamped surfaces and for decorative field treatments adjacent to the crossings. Pewter is a neutral grey with warm undertones that reads as slate or aged stone under BC overcast light. It is a palette choice that reinforces the heritage-streetscape intent without committing to a saturated colour that would date more quickly.

The combined install gives Mission a downtown pavement treatment that reads as intentional and considered, with durability profiles that match the municipality's long-cycle capital-planning horizon.

## What the Corridor Reads Like Now

Downtown Mission now has a pavement treatment that matches the scale and ambition of its heritage architecture. A visitor walking the corridor registers the stamped texture underfoot, the colour field across the surface, and the crosswalk treatments at the intersections — and the read is of a downtown that has been curated rather than a downtown that has been left to wear out.

From the District's perspective, the install is one piece of a multi-year commitment. Facade grants, streetfurniture replacement, and public-realm programming sit alongside the pavement work. The combined effect is what restores a downtown's trajectory, and the pavement carries its portion of that work for the fifteen-plus-year service horizon of the StreetBond coating.
