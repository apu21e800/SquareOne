In collaboration with Musqueam, we installed a new original crosswalk design on Granville Street — artwork by Musqueam artist Robyn Sparrow, rendered in TrafficPatterns preformed thermoplastic and fused into the asphalt surface.

The installation was timed to coincide with National Indigenous Peoples Day. It's the kind of project that demonstrates what surface design can do when it's treated as a vehicle for community recognition rather than just traffic management.

## Working with Artist Robyn Sparrow

The design was developed by Robyn Sparrow and translated into the precise colour separations and sheet geometry required for TrafficPatterns installation. This is where the technical and the cultural requirements meet: the artwork has to survive the conversion from digital design to pre-cut thermoplastic sheets without losing the integrity of the original design intent.

We worked closely with the Musqueam project team on the colour specifications, scale, and placement so that the finished crosswalk reads as it was designed — not as a compromise between the artist's vision and what the material system can deliver.

## TrafficPatterns for Public Art Crosswalks

Pre-formed thermoplastic is the correct product choice for crosswalks with cultural or institutional significance. The design is cut from CAD artwork, so geometric precision is maintained regardless of complexity. The colours are formulated to retain saturation for years rather than months. And the installation is fast enough that the intersection can be closed for a single work day rather than a multi-day disruption.

The Granville Street installation joins a portfolio of First Nations partnership crosswalks we've completed across BC — from Squamish to Musqueam to the Sunshine Coast. Each one is a permanent statement in the public realm.
