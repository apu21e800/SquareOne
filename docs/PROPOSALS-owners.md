# Proposals for the owners — nothing here ships until blessed
Per docs/LETTER.md: the offer is a business decision; making it
irresistible is ours. Vern carries these to Jan & Gord.

## 1. The first-step offer (beat 4) — pick one primary
A. THE STRAIGHT-ANSWER SITE WALK (recommended primary)
   "We'll walk your driveway, tell you honestly whether your asphalt can
   be stamped — and if it can't, we'll tell you that too." Costs a crew
   visit; converts because the honesty IS the demonstration. Fits the
   voice: We walk the site before we quote it.
B. PATTERN & COLOUR CONSULT
   Sit-down (or video) with the sample chips and pattern book against
   photos of their house. Low cost, high desire-building; natural
   companion to A as the follow-up step.
C. "WHAT WOULD MINE LOOK LIKE" PHOTO MOCK
   Homeowner sends a photo of their driveway; S1 returns it re-surfaced
   in a chosen pattern/colour. Highest wow per dollar; needs §11-grade
   honesty labelling (a visualization, never presented as a completed
   install) and owner comfort with turnaround expectations.
Recommendation: A primary CTA on /driveways, B offered inside A's
follow-up, C piloted quietly on ten leads before it goes on the site.

## 2. "STILL STANDING." — the revisit program
What it needs from the owners: a list of 6-10 installs from ~2005/2012/
2018 they're proud of; permission from those property owners; one
photographer day per region (or crew phone shots to start, upgraded
later). What it yields: the beat-3 section nobody can fake, per-project
"Installed 2012 - photographed 2026" pairs, search content with no
possible copycat, and pride. Ship as a Projects filter + a homepage
strip + one journal post per revisit.

## 3. Photography shot list (beats 2 & 3) — for the next real shoot
- Before/after pairs: SAME angle, same lens height, marked positions;
  driveway minimum set: street view, approach, texture close-up.
- The crew: real people, real PPE, hands and faces (with individual
  permission), day-1 prep / day-2 stamping / cure walk-through sequence.
- The materials: chips, pattern mats, the rig — photographed like craft
  tools, not equipment inventory.
No stock, ever. Nothing from this list is generated imagery (§11 governs
the vapour-blasting exception only).
