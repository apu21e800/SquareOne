TrafficPatternsXD at Brighouse Station

By the Richmond install crew · June 3, 2026
Richmond · TrafficPatternsXD · 2019

Brighouse Station anchors the Richmond end of the Canada Line, and the plaza in front of it never really empties. Buses pull through all day, bikes cut the corners, and every surface decision gets tested by rubber within the week.

The City wanted a patterned surface that would organize the plaza without paint lines, and that could take turning buses without scuffing into a blur by winter.

Why the XD gauge

Standard TrafficPatterns handles crosswalks and medians well. Where buses turn, we specify TrafficPatternsXD: a heavier-gauge preformed sheet made for intersections and plazas. It fuses the same way and reads the same from the curb; the difference shows up three winters later.

A plaza in front of a station is an intersection that never gets a night off

Panel layout

The pattern arrived as 34 factory-cut panels. We dry-laid the full field on a Sunday morning, walked it with the City’s project engineer, and only then brought out the heat. Fusing ran across two overnight windows, sequenced so the bus loop stayed open.

Richmond · TrafficPatternsXD · 2019
Dry-laying panels before the torches come out.

What we would repeat

  • Dry-lay the full field before fusing anything.
  • Sequence the work around bus service rather than against it.
  • Leave spare panels with the owner for future utility cuts.

The plaza opened on schedule both mornings. Seven years on, the pattern still holds its edges where the buses turn, which is the only review that matters to us.
